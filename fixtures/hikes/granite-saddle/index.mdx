Granite Saddle is the big day in the Cedar Range: a lollipop loop that climbs to First Pass, contours past Tarn Lake, crosses a scree field and tops out on the wide granite slabs of the saddle before dropping back through meadows. It's 11.6 miles and 3,400 feet of gain, most of it in one sustained climb, so start early. Going clockwise puts the steep switchbacks on fresh legs and the long, gentle descent at the end.

## The route

<Step waypoint="trailhead">
Arrive by 7:30 on weekends. If the lot is full, park on the shoulder of FR 4410 below the last switchback; don't block the gate.
</Step>

<Step waypoint="ranger-station">
The guard station is usually staffed Friday to Sunday in summer, and the rangers post current snow and water conditions on the porch board. It's the last reliable place to report a problem in person.
</Step>

<Step waypoint="loop-junction">
The stem from here is easy, rolling forest. Note the junction's shape (four ways, big hemlock on the corner): you'll arrive back here from the right in about seven hours.

Count them if it helps; the number is painted on a small blaze at every fifth turn. The Cedar Range view comes at switchback 22.

## Water and bail-outs

There's water at miles 1.6, 5.0 and 8.0, but the 8.0 spring dries up in late summer, so treat Tarn Lake as your last sure fill-up. The Upper Basin trail (3.9 mi) and the fire road (8.8 mi) are the only ways off the loop. Between them, the safest way out is the way you came.
